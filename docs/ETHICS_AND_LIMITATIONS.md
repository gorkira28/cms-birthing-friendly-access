# Ethics, limitations, and responsible use

## What the analysis can support

This project can identify state-level patterns that deserve closer review. It is suitable for portfolio demonstration, exploratory program planning, and generating follow-up questions.

## What it cannot support

- **No clinical ranking:** the designation is a structural attestation, not a risk-adjusted maternal outcome score.
- **No access conclusion:** state density does not measure drive time, service areas, bed capacity, delivery volume, network participation, or whether labor and delivery units are open.
- **No causal inference:** the positive relationship between density and Critical Access Hospital share is ecological and descriptive.
- **No individual guidance:** results should not be used to choose a delivery hospital or replace advice from clinicians and health plans.
- **No blank-to-no assumption:** blank designation values are not interpreted as clinical failure.
- **Population mismatch:** women ages 15–44 are a reproducible denominator, but this category does not represent everyone who may become pregnant and includes many people who will not seek maternity care.
- **Geographic scope:** Puerto Rico is reported separately because a comparable denominator is not contained in the chosen Census state file. Other territories are not analyzed.
- **Time alignment:** CMS reflects a July 2026 modification date; the denominator is July 1, 2025. The one-year difference should be considered in close comparisons.

## Privacy and equity

All records are public facility-level or state-level data. No patient-level information or PHI is used. State averages may conceal rural, racial, socioeconomic, and within-state inequities; an equitable next step would add county-level travel time, birth volume, payer mix, and outcome measures with appropriate suppression and uncertainty handling.
